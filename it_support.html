01 Context

A wall of misunderstanding between IT and employees

When employees need support, they shouldn’t have to be tech-savvy. They don’t want “tickets”, they have problems. What if they could just describe them, in their own words?

A conversational AI assistant would only work if people understand what it can do, trust its answers, and feel understood, whatever words they use.

02 What I did

From user research to an omnichannel AI experience

I led the design and research strategy from start to finish:

  • Customer-facing workshops to align stakeholders on needs and priorities
  • AI design principles to guide how the assistant behaves
  • The entire user journey across platforms (Teams and web portal): service design, conversation design and UX/UI design
  • 20+ usability testing sessions after the beta release
  • Accessibility documentation and design handover to the development team

03 Results

Live in 10 countries, and still improving

The assistant is already rolled out in 10 countries, with 15% adoption so far. Incidents shifted left by 50%, and calls to the service desk dropped by 80%.

It keeps improving, shaped by what users say and do, with the design principles and journeys as a shared base for new features.
